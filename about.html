I work on normative and computational theory of how neural systems build structured internal models of the world, and on the same question in artificial systems.

My thesis derives a quotient law for predictive learning. A recurrent predictive network trained on egocentric observations represents state space only up to the symmetry subgroup whose actions are indistinguishable from self-motion. That law predicts when place fields should repeat across identical rooms.

I am also interested in successor-style and factorised representations, in the TEM and SR lineage, and in the developmental question of when representational structure becomes usable during training. That second question covers critical-period effects in language models, and the gap between what a model represents and what it actually uses at query time.

I am looking to do theory-first computational neuroscience in close contact with experimental data, not wet-lab work.

Perseus

Chief Scientist · Remote · 2025 to present

I set and lead the research agenda across four lines, and supervise a small research team and external collaborations.

  • Coding world models. Learned models of a codebase and its execution behaviour that an agent can roll forward and plan against, rather than retrieving text. Related architectural work adapts TEM-style structural priors as a persistent, slot-structured state on a transformer backbone.
  • Developmental pretraining. The experimental programme on when capabilities become acquirable, retainable, and recoverable during training, from a small controlled model up to OLMo-2 continued pretraining, under a preregistration protocol with predictions filed before launch.
  • Interpretability. The instrument stack for the routing and binding programme: Jacobian-based binding probes, path patching, and per-checkpoint developmental sweeps. I ran an adversarial audit of our own corpus that retracted several previously published numbers.
  • Evaluation. I direct BioBench, an external-facing benchmark for biological mechanism reasoning. It uses a symbolic verifier that certifies or rejects a chain of reasoning, rather than scoring accuracy.
